<div class="services-intro">
  <p>This page borrows a framework from effective altruism — the practice of evaluating interventions by <strong>scale, neglectedness, tractability, and counterfactual impact</strong> — and turns it on my own work. I'm not a grantmaker allocating a budget across causes. But the same discipline applies to a career: it's worth asking not just "what did I do," but "did it matter, was anyone else doing it, could it actually be fixed, and would it have happened without me."</p>
</div>

## The Framework

<div class="impact-framework">

  <div class="impact-pillar">
    <h4>Scale</h4>
    <p>How many people or decisions does this actually touch — a single report, or a national dataset? Bigger isn't automatically better, but it's the first question.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="impact-pillar">
    <h4>Neglectedness</h4>
    <p>Is this a well-resourced problem with ten people already on it, or is it the unglamorous work — records management, migration QA, governance — that nobody wants to own?</p>
  </div>

  <div class="impact-pillar">
    <h4>Tractability</h4>
    <p>Can this actually be solved with the tools, time, and access available, or is it a problem that just absorbs effort without ever resolving?</p>
  </div>

  <div class="impact-pillar">
    <h4>Counterfactual Impact</h4>
    <p>What would have happened without this piece of work — would the gap have gone unnoticed, or would someone else have caught it anyway?</p>
  </div>

</div>

## 📊 Royal Media Services — Evidence for Decisions That Affect Real Programmes

**2024 – Present**

**The problem.** NGOs, government agencies, health organisations, and businesses were making decisions about programmes and campaigns — some of which affect entire communities — often without rigorous evidence of whether the underlying intervention was actually working.

**What I did.** Ran randomised control trials and implementation research to test whether interventions were delivered as intended, with fidelity, and with potential for cost-effectiveness. Led qualitative research (focus groups, interviews, ethnography) and quantitative segmentation to understand the populations these programmes were meant to serve. Built predictive models and produced reports, briefs, and case studies that carried these findings to the people making the actual funding and strategy decisions.

**Scale & neglectedness.** RCTs and implementation research are resource-intensive and easy to skip in favour of simpler output metrics — which is exactly why skipping them is where a lot of wasted programme spending comes from. This is genuinely under-resourced work relative to how much it should inform decisions.

**Counterfactual impact.** Without this evidence, clients would be deciding whether to continue, scale, or cut a programme based on activity metrics (attendance, reach) rather than whether it changed anything for the people it targeted.

## 🚗 Autochek Africa — The Data Backbone Behind Affordable Car Financing

**2022 – 2024**

**The problem.** A Business Intelligence function spanning Sub-Saharan Africa had projects, reports, and data pipelines with no single owner accountable end-to-end — meaning errors could travel from a source system, through a migration, into a dashboard, and into a financing decision without anyone catching them.

**What I did.** Owned every BI project across the organisation: roadmap, sprint planning, requirements gathering, and end-to-end QA from source system to Power BI. Closed data migration gaps by working directly with analysts, data engineers, and developers across every Sub-Saharan African market Autochek operated in. Built internal tools for loan management, fleet management, and data governance & audit, and authored the BI Standard Operating Procedures and role-based access model the team still works from.

**Scale & neglectedness.** This is infrastructure work — governance, QA, SOPs — that doesn't show up in a product demo but is exactly where accuracy either holds or breaks down at scale, across an entire region.

**Counterfactual impact.** Without a single owner running QA end-to-end and closing migration gaps directly with engineers, inaccurate data had a much longer runway to reach a real lending or fleet decision before anyone noticed.

## 🏥 National Hospital Insurance Fund — The Groundwork Behind Health Policy

**2021**

**The problem.** Regional visibility into insurance coverage depended on manual, paper-based records — exactly the kind of unglamorous groundwork that determines whether Universal Health Coverage planning is built on accurate information or guesswork.

**What I did.** Data entry, cleaning, visualisation, and regional report creation on insurance coverage; SQL-based modelling and Python scripts to support the analysis; and supporting the secretary with records management so the underlying documentation stayed accurate and retrievable.

**Scale & neglectedness.** Data entry and records management are the least prestigious parts of any data pipeline, and also the parts where a single error propagates into every report built on top of it. This is exactly the kind of neglected work the framework above is meant to surface.

**Counterfactual impact.** Reports built on cleaner regional data fed directly into national UHC policy readiness planning — decisions that affect coverage at population scale.

### What I Won't Claim

In keeping with the standard above, I'd rather be precise than impressive. I don't currently have an independent, audited evaluation of my own work's downstream outcomes — most of it fed into decisions made by other people, in organisations where I wasn't the one tracking the final result. Where I can point to something concrete — a system built, a trial run, a gap closed, a market covered — I've said so above. Where I can't yet quantify the downstream effect precisely, I've described the mechanism honestly instead of attaching a number to it that I can't stand behind.
